Insects

Common household pests vary in lifespan, reproduction cycles, and treatment strategies. Below is a quick reference for the insects we most commonly treat.

Termites

  • Lifespan: 1 to 10+ years depending on species and colony role.
  • Lifecycle: Egg → nymph → worker/soldier/reproductive, with colonies growing rapidly in warm, moist wood.
  • Treatment: Soil treatments, wood treatments, moisture control, and structural repairs to remove conditions that attract them.

Roaches

  • Lifespan: 3 to 12 months, depending on species and environment.
  • Lifecycle: Egg case → nymphs → adult, with fast reproduction when food and moisture are available.
  • Treatment: Baiting, crack-and-crevice treatments, sanitation, and exclusion of moisture and food access.

Bed Bugs

  • Lifespan: About 4 to 6 months, though some can survive longer under ideal conditions.
  • Lifecycle: Egg → nymph → adult, with multiple feeding cycles and rapid hiding in mattresses, bed frames, and cracks.
  • Treatment: Heat treatments, targeted insecticide applications, and thorough inspection of bedding, furniture, and surrounding crevices.

Mites

  • Lifespan: Typically a few weeks to several months, depending on species and host conditions.
  • Lifecycle: Egg → larva → nymph → adult, often living in dust, fabrics, or on warm-blooded hosts.
  • Treatment: Deep cleaning, laundering textiles, vacuuming, dust control, and targeted indoor treatment to reduce harborage areas.

Fleas

  • Lifespan: 2 to 3 months on average, though they can live longer in warm indoor conditions.
  • Lifecycle: Egg → larva → pupa → adult, with rapid outbreaks when pets bring them indoors.
  • Treatment: Pet treatment, carpet and upholstery treatment, vacuuming, and follow-up applications to break the life cycle.

Ants

  • Lifespan: Worker ants usually live a few weeks to a few months; queens can live several years.
  • Lifecycle: Egg → larva → pupa → adult, with established colonies spreading through trails and nesting sites.
  • Treatment: Bait stations, perimeter treatment, crack sealing, and moisture reduction to disrupt colony access.

Beetles

  • Lifespan: A few months to several years depending on species and food source.
  • Lifecycle: Egg → larva → pupa → adult, with many species infesting stored goods, wood, or fabrics.
  • Treatment: Source removal, sanitation, sealing entry points, and targeted insecticide or fumigation when infestations are severe.

Flies

  • Lifespan: Usually a few days to several weeks depending on species and environment.
  • Lifecycle: Egg → larva → pupa → adult, with rapid breeding around garbage, drains, manure, or decaying organic material.
  • Treatment: Sanitation, drain and trash management, exclusion, and targeted insect control to stop breeding sources.
